# Style pack: <name>

Template. Copy with `python tools/packs.py --new <id>`, then replace every section with what the references show. Write what you actually see, in concrete terms a scene author can follow (sizes, colours, line widths, what is never done). This file overrides `references/craft-bible.md` wherever the two differ.

## Source
Where the look comes from: reference channels / screenshots in `refs/`, the user's own description, or both. Note what was asked to keep and what to change. Never copy a competitor's characters, mascots, logos or titles: take the style traits only.

## The look in one paragraph
Line work, fill, shading, light, texture, level of detail, overall mood.

## Characters
Family (`characters.family` in style.json), proportions and construction, outline or no outline, how faces and expressions look, how characters are told apart, scale in wide / medium / close shots.

## Sets and environments
Detail level, perspective (flat / 2.5D / isometric), outlines, gradients or flat fills, textures, recurring elements.

## Light and colour
Which moods the pack defines and when to use each, where light comes from, how shadows are drawn, accent colours.

## Camera and editing
How much the camera moves (from locked-off to constant drift), typical shot length, preferred transitions, letterbox or not.

## Shot mix
Which shot types this look uses and roughly how often (see the generic vocabulary in the craft bible). Signature moments, if any.

## Graphics and typography
Fonts per role, how numbers, labels, maps and charts look, captions style.

## Variants
One line per colour variant in style.json.

## Preferred story format
See `references/story-formats.md`.

## Don't
What would break this look.
