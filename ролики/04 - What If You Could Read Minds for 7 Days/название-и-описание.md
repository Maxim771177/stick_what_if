## Название (40 симв., выбрано владельцем)
What If You Could Read Minds for 7 Days?

## Описание
It's Monday morning, and a small cloud floats above every head in school. Inside each one is what that person is thinking right now. Rocco, the toughest kid around, begs for a friend. Chase, the dodgeball captain, wonders if anyone likes him. And Zoe's cloud says, "Don't look at him. Look at him. Don't."

What if you could read minds for 7 days? Leon has a list: find out if Zoe likes him, become the coolest kid in school, and learn what his dad really thinks of him. Day one is all perks: he dodges every ball, knows the teacher's next question, and gets a seat at the coolest table. Then the week keeps going, and the clouds start showing him things he never wanted to see.

We cover: why one person has about 6,000 separate thoughts a day (Tseng and Poppenk, Nature Communications, 2020), why a mind is somewhere else almost half the time (Killingsworth and Gilbert, Science, 2010, about 47% of the time), why nearly everyone has random, unwanted thoughts and what that really means (a study of students in 13 countries, 2014), how scientists in Texas turned brain scans into rough sentences (Tang and colleagues, Nature Neuroscience, 2023, after about 16 hours in a scanner, and only with the person's cooperation), and the illusion of transparency: people believe others can read their feelings far more than they really can (Gilovich, Medvec and Savitsky, 1998).
