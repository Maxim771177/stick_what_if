What If Your Reflexes Were 10x Faster?

## Описание
Monday, third period. Ten dodgeballs fly at one kid, and every single one misses. Leon didn't run. He just started moving a fraction of a second sooner than everyone else. So what if your reflexes were ten times faster?

Here's what it is, in three rules: the gap between seeing something and starting to move shrinks from a quarter of a second to a fortieth; you start sooner, but you don't move faster; and the world isn't in slow motion, everybody else just seems to be loading. Then come the perks (a pancake, a whack-a-mole machine, a tripping lunch tray, a game of tag played at a walk) and the problems nobody warns you about.

We cover: how long a normal reaction takes, about a quarter of a second (Woods and colleagues, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, 2015); the ruler-drop test, 25 centimeters against 3 millimeters; why flies see a flickering world and why lights that look steady to us would flicker for Leon (Healy and colleagues, Animal Behaviour, 2013; humans stop seeing flicker at around 60 flashes a second); why a brain that is 2% of your body weight burns about 20% of your energy (Raichle and Gusnard, PNAS, 2002), with the extra cost of a faster brain being our guess; and the real World Athletics rule that anything under 0.1 seconds counts as a false start, the one that disqualified Devon Allen at the 2022 World Championships.
