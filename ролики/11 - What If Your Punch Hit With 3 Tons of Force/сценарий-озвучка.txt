Leon hits eight times harder. Rule three. The force follows effort. Even a tap at a tenth of your strength lands with six hundred pounds. Your bones and your skin can take all of it. [matter-of-fact] Problem three. Three tons sounds unstoppable. It isn't. On Monday, Leon is mad about a bad quiz grade, and he walks past the steel door of the equipment shed behind the gym. He punches it. The door rings like a bell. Leon yelps and shakes his hand, and the door has a dent no bigger than a coin. Here's why. What matters is how much force you use, and also how small a spot it lands on. Six thousand pounds on a fist-sized spot, about three square inches, is roughly two thousand pounds on every square inch. A wooden door gives up long before that. Ordinary structural steel doesn't start to bend until about thirty-six thousand pounds on every square inch, eighteen times more. So Leon can wreck a fence, a dumpster and a brick mailbox, but a steel door just laughs at him. [sarcastic] A kid who felt like a god for one weekend now has a sore hand and a dent he'll have to explain to the custodian. [energetic] Coach Dunn looks at the door and tells him something that sticks. "Never fight the whole door. Find what's holding it. A latch, a hinge, a bolt.
