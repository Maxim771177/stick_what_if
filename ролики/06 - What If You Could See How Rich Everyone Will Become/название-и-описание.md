## Название (46 симв., утверждено в сценарии)
What If You Could See How Rich Everyone Will Become?

## Описание
Tuesday, 7:52 AM, Maple Street. Leon drops his last two dollars in a paper cup, and a glowing number appears above the man holding it: $6,310,000,000. Above Leon's own head: $61,400. So what if you could see how rich everyone will become?

Here's how it works, in three rules: the number is the most money a person will ever have if nothing changes; it changes the second something else does; and it shows money and nothing else, not how, not when, and not whether anyone is happy. Then come the perks (a bigger allowance, a lemonade stand that only serves seven-digit people, the perfect group project partner, a class election) and the problems nobody warns you about.

We cover: why a number is only the end of a long road (the writer Morgan Housel calculates that Warren Buffett built about 99% of his fortune after his 50th birthday); why telling someone "you've already won" can make them stop trying (Mueller and Dweck, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 1998: fifth graders praised for being smart did worse on the next round than kids praised for effort); and the real scale of homelessness in the United States (about 771,000 people on a single night in January 2024, HUD annual report).
